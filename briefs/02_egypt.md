# 02 — Egyptian Wall Painting · c. 1300 BCE · film 11–17 s
**Looks like:** a New Kingdom Theban tomb wall (Nebamun, Nakht, Menna): tempera on gypsum plaster.
**Substrate:** warm cream limestone plaster — fine cracks, chips showing mud-straw underlayer, a lost patch or two.
The **artist's preliminary grid** in thin red ochre is faintly visible across the background (18-square canon grid).
**Composition:** registers on heavy black/red ground lines. Main register on the road (y = 800): the pelican's lane.
Upper register: a procession of offering bearers (composite view: profile head and legs, frontal eye and shoulders;
red-brown skin, white kilts, black wigs, broad collars) carrying lotus, fowl, jars. A seated noble couple before an
offering table at one end. A marsh frieze (papyrus, ducks) or kheker frieze along the top. Below the road: a base band
of coloured stripes (dado). Flat mineral colours: red ochre, yellow ochre, Egyptian blue, malachite green, white, black;
red-brown contour lines.
**Hieroglyphic panel:** a framed column/cartouche panel that *reads* "The Pelican Test": spell it with drawn uniliteral
signs — p (square mat), l/r (mouth), k (basket with handle), n (water zigzag), then t (bread loaf), s (folded cloth),
t — closing with a determinative: a little pelican-on-bicycle glyph. Draw the glyphs; no font needed. Beside it the
era title card (zh / en / date) in a painted label band.
**Pelican:** Egyptian canon: flat fills, red-brown outline, frontal almond eye with kohl line, wing with coloured
feather bands like a Horus falcon, broad collar; bicycle in black and gold with **six-spoked chariot wheels**.
**Gag (required):** slow the action to a **freeze as the pelican strikes a conventional wall-painting pose.** Suggested
`profile: [[0,0],[2.0,0.46],[2.5,0.5],[3.6,0.5],[4.3,0.56],[6,1]]`. From t ≈ 2.2 ease into the pose and hold it
t = 2.5–3.6: neck erect, beak level, one wing raised in the gesture of adoration (`p.wave = 1`), pedalling stopped.
While frozen, the red canon grid lights up around the figure (it fits the squares exactly), and the offering bearers
are already facing it, arms raised. Then the grid fades and it rides on as if nothing happened.
**Before / after:** bearers mid-procession → afterwards the nearest bearer has turned his head to look after it.
**Music here:** Egyptian harp.
