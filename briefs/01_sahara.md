# 01 — Saharan Rock Art · c. 4000 BCE, Tassili style · film 5–11 s   (lead style — painted first)
**Looks like:** a Tassili n'Ajjer pastoral-period shelter wall: warm sandstone with desert varnish, water streaks,
bedding planes, pits and cracks; figures in red and yellow ochre, dark brown and kaolin white, weathered so the grain
of the rock shows through the pigment.
**Composition:** a herd of long-horned cattle (red, piebald, yellow, a dark bull, a white-outlined cow, a calf) and
slender herders with staffs and a bow in the upper two-thirds; negative hand stencils top right; a faint frieze of
small running hunters under the ledge. The road is a natural ledge where one sandstone bed steps out over the next,
picked out with a dotted trail of white spots.
**Pelican:** an ochre silhouette with kaolin pouch, wing bars and wheel dots, repainted every frame and weathered by a
flake mask anchored to the rock.
**Gag:** "Herders and their cattle stop to watch." The cattle graze until the pelican enters, then lift their heads one
after another (nearest the left frame first) and follow it; on the downbeat of bar 2 the herders' arms shoot out to
point; a child waves. Afterwards they stay looking down the road, then drift back to grazing.
**Technique:** 2D rock texture; flat pigment silhouettes + p5.brush (`ochre`, `pastel`, `charcoal`) contours and rubbed
texture baked on white and multiplied in; heads and arms are separate sprites.
**Music here:** hand drums and chanting.
