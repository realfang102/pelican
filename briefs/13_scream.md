# 13 — Munch, *The Scream* · 1893 · film 77–83 s
**Looks like:** tempera and crayon on cardboard: a blood-orange sky in long undulating bands, a blue-black fjord
swirling in the same rhythm, and a bridge with a plank deck and railing; streaky, dry, visibly scrubbed strokes with
bare board between them.
**Composition:** the bridge deck is our road (y = 800): planks and the long railing running across the foreground, with
the picture's steep diagonal suggested in the railing posts and the plank joints converging to the left. Behind it the
fjord, a hint of town and boats, the wavy sky. Two small dark figures in top hats stand far off on the bridge at the
left. Centre-right, on the deck behind the riding line: **the figure** — the hairless head, hands clapped to its
cheeks, mouth an open oval.
**Gag (required): "the pelican rides across the bridge, and the screaming figure becomes a startled passer-by."**
Before the pelican arrives the figure screams as painted and the whole landscape undulates with it (shift the sky and
fjord bands in slow sine waves — draw the baked sky in horizontal slices with offsets). As the pelican comes level
(land it on a beat in bar 2), the scream simply stops: the hands come down from the cheeks, the mouth closes to a small
"o", the head turns to follow the bicycle — an ordinary person on a bridge, mildly surprised. The landscape calms
with it. After the pelican has gone the figure looks after it, then slowly raises its hands again.
**Pelican:** in Munch's manner — wavering contour, flat murky colour (bone white, ochre beak, blue-black bicycle),
strokes that echo the sky's rhythm. Per-frame wavering lines with fixed seeds.
**Title card:** scrawled in crayon in a lower corner (zh / en / date), high contrast against the deck.
**Music here:** strings that suddenly veer out of tune and shriek (around the middle of bar 1 into bar 2), then recover.
