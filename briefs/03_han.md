# 03 — Han Dynasty Stone Relief Rubbing · c. 100 CE · film 17–23 s  (ink toolkit: `K.ink.rubbingize`)
**Looks like:** an ink rubbing (拓片) of an Eastern Han pictorial stone (Wu Family Shrines, Yinan): **white figures and
lines on black.** The black is ink dabbed through paper — mottled, starved in places, with paper wrinkles and the
ghost of the stone's chisel striations; the figures are bare paper.
**Technique:** draw the figures as WHITE silhouettes on a transparent layer, with details as thin black incised lines
cut back into them (manes, wheel spokes, robe folds), then pass the layer through `K.ink.rubbingize()`.
Borders: bands of lozenges, cloud scrolls or rope pattern between registers, as on the stones.
**Composition:** two or three registers. The road register carries a **procession of horses and carriages** (車馬出行):
two-wheeled canopied carriages with seated officials, prancing horses with arched necks, outriders, runners with
staffs, all heading right. Upper register: a pavilion with seated host and kneeling guests, a tree with birds, an
archer; mythical creatures in the top band. Stiff, formal, rhythmic.
**Pelican:** the same carved language: flat white silhouette, black incised feather and spoke lines — it should look
like it was always part of the stone.
**Gag (required):** the **pelican on its bicycle slips into the procession** — there is a gap between two carriages
and for a beat or two in bar 2 it trots along in line as one more vehicle (ease the `profile` so it idles in the gap
around t = 2.5–3.5). Horses turn their heads; the driver behind lowers his reins; a kneeling attendant by the road bows
to it as he would to any official. The procession's horses may step in place at a stiff 2–4 poses per second.
**Title card:** an inscription cartouche like the stones' labels — vertical white-on-black carved characters for the zh
title, with en/date in a carved label; plus a red collector's seal on the rubbing's margin.
**Music here:** bronze chime bells.
