# 04 — Tang Dynasty Baimiao Line Drawing · c. 750 · film 23–29 s  (ink toolkit)
**Looks like:** plain-line (白描) figure painting in the manner attributed to Wu Daozi — "Wu's drapery in the wind"
(吳帶當風); think of the *Eighty-Seven Immortals* scroll. **Ink outline only**: no colour, no wash (a breath of pale
ink in hair knots at most). Long, elastic "orchid-leaf" and "iron-wire" lines whose width swells and thins.
**Substrate:** aged silk or sized paper (`K.ink.silk` / `K.ink.xuan`), a little darkened; finish with `K.ink.weave`.
**Composition:** a celestial procession along a balustraded bridge or cloud terrace (the road, y = 800): immortals and
attendants with tall hair knots, banners, feather fans, canopies and tray offerings; lotus balustrade posts; scrolling
clouds below and behind. Everything drawn in line, densely where robes overlap, with generous bare ground above.
**Pelican:** pure line: every feather a long drawn stroke; bicycle in even iron-wire line; knock out the ground inside
the bird with ground colour so lines behind do not show through. It wears long celestial sashes.
**Gag (required): "the pelican's feathers and streamers billow in the wind."** On the downbeat of bar 2 a gust arrives:
the pelican's streamers lengthen into long rippling sashes (draw your own ribbons from `pose.pt.neckBase` — several,
120–300 units, travelling waves), crest and tail feathers stream, and every sash, banner and sleeve in the procession
lifts and ripples in the same wind, a beat behind. In bar 3 it settles. These moving lines are per-frame
`K.ink.line`/`K.brushStroke` strokes with fixed seeds; static figures are baked with p5.brush (`inkFine`, `ink`).
**Before / after:** still air, sashes hanging → afterwards a banner still swaying.
**Title card:** a colophon in the upper corner — bold kai-style zh, en and date beneath, a red seal or two.
**Music here:** pipa.
